## Summary

A method and architecture for drawing electrical power from the ambient gravitational wave background using arrays of thin film silicon elements, where photon excitation acts as a tuning mechanism to bring each element into resonant coupling with target gravitational wave frequencies. The photon is not the energy source — it is the tuning signal. The gravitational wave background is the energy source.

---

## Background

The Gertsenshtein effect (1962) established that electromagnetic radiation and gravitational radiation can interconvert in the presence of a strong magnetic field. This is a bidirectional process: photons can produce gravitational waves, and gravitational waves can produce photons.

Subsequent work in high-frequency gravitational wave (HFGW) research has explored thin film semiconductor materials as transducer elements. Specifically, arrays of thin film silicon wafers have been demonstrated to exhibit photon-to-gravitational-wave coupling behaviour (Gary Stephenson et al., demonstrated at EarthTech International, Austin TX).

## The Reframe: Photon as Tuning Signal, Not Energy Source

Prior interpretations of thin film silicon gravitational coupling have framed the photon as one half of an energy conversion pair — photons in, gravitational waves out, or vice versa. This disclosure proposes a materially different model:

**The photon does not create or become the gravitational wave. The photon excites the thin film silicon element to a resonant frequency at which that element couples to the ambient gravitational wave background.**

Under this model, the silicon chip functions as an antenna, not a transducer. The photon input is a tuning cost — small. The energy drawn is from the pre-existing gravitational wave background — potentially large, and completely unmetered by any existing energy infrastructure.

This reframing has the following implications:

1. **The energy balance is not closed by the photon input alone.** A correctly tuned thin film silicon array exposed to photon excitation at the right frequency will output more electrical energy than the photon input accounts for — not because thermodynamics is violated, but because the gravitational background is an additional input that conventional energy accounting ignores.

2. **Photovoltaic cells have been doing this as a byproduct since 1954.** Any thin film silicon junction exposed to photons of the correct frequency is incidentally tuned to gravitational resonance. The gravitational component of the output has been present in every solar cell measurement ever taken and has been categorised as noise, efficiency variation, or unexplained anomaly.

3. **"Anomalous" solid state power outputs are explicable under this model.** Devices reporting electrical output in excess of photon input — including solid state energy research conducted by Harold "Sonny" White and others — are consistent with unaccounted gravitational wave background coupling. The anomaly is not a violation; it is an unmeasured input.

## Architecture

### Single Element

A thin film silicon element of specified thickness and doping profile, illuminated by photons of a tuning frequency `f_tune`, enters resonant coupling with gravitational waves at a target frequency `f_gw`. The relationship between `f_tune` and `f_gw` is a function of the material geometry and the coupling coefficient of the Gertsenshtein mechanism in silicon.

The element outputs a current proportional to the gravitational wave energy density at `f_gw` multiplied by the coupling efficiency.

### Array Architecture

Multiple elements arranged in a phased array, with software-controlled excitation timing and phase offsets across elements, provide:

- **Directionality:** Phase offset between elements steers the reception angle, analogous to phased array radar.
- **Frequency selectivity:** Tuning frequency sweep across elements selects target gravitational frequency bands.
- **Amplitude scaling:** Output current scales with number of elements. Large arrays compound draw from the background.
- **Software-defined operation:** The array is reconfigurable in software. Changing the excitation pattern changes the reception profile without hardware modification.

This is the gravitational equivalent of a software-defined radio (SDR) — a programmable antenna system operating in a previously unmonitored spectrum.

### Wireless Power Transmission Variant

A transmit array (driven electrically to emit gravitational waves at a target frequency) paired with a receive array (tuned to that frequency) constitutes a wireless power transmission system. The transmission medium is spacetime itself. The signal passes through all conventional shielding materials — Faraday cages, walls, water, earth — without attenuation, because none of these materials interact with gravitational radiation at these frequencies.

## Relation to Known Prior Art

- **Gertsenshtein (1962):** Established bidirectional EM/gravitational coupling in magnetic fields. This disclosure extends the mechanism to thin film silicon without requiring an external magnetic field, and reframes the photon role from energy partner to tuning signal.

- **Robert Baker, Fangyu Li et al.:** HFGW detector proposals using microwave cavities and Gaussian beam interference. This disclosure differs in using thin film silicon arrays as the coupling element and in the tuning-antenna model.

- **Harold "Sonny" White (EarthTech International / NASA Eagleworks):** Solid state energy devices reporting anomalous output. This disclosure provides a candidate mechanism for those observations.

- **Gary Stephenson et al. (EarthTech International):** Demonstrated thin film silicon wafer arrays for photon-gravitational wave coupling. This disclosure proposes the alternative model (tuning, not conversion) and the array architectures that follow from it.
